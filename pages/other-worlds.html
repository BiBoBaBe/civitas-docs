Three more, quickly. None of them is built. Each one names the single piece of machinery that would carry it.

Vetrograd, 1961

A city cut in half by a wall that went up eight years ago. You are a translator at a trade delegation, which is a job that requires you to sit in rooms with people who are being watched, and to remember which of them knows what. Everybody you deal with is running a second version of themselves and most of them are not very good at it.

Anneliese runs a bar two streets from the crossing and wants an exit visa for her brother. It costs her the only thing she has that anybody wants: she hears everything, and she has so far told nobody. Kovacs is your opposite number and wants a posting home before the winter, which costs him a report about you that he has been putting off for five weeks. Dr Lenz has a flat with a good lock and a spare room, and wants nothing at all, which is the most frightening thing about him.

The safehouse is a first-floor flat above a stationer's with two doors and a landing you can hear from the stairwell. Something has already gone wrong. A courier who was meant to arrive on Tuesday did not, and three people have now told you three different versions of why, one of which has your name in it.

The mechanic: doors, and who can hear. A conversation at a table is between two people. The rest of the room gets a roll, and what they can win is that something was said, never the words. A shut door hides what happened and cannot hide that it shut. That is most of a genre.

Dunmerrow

Eleven hundred people, one high street, three pubs, and a body in the reservoir. You have come back after nineteen years because your aunt died and left you her house, and nobody here has quite decided whether you are local.

The publican wants the bypass to go through, because it would double his trade, and it costs him that he cannot say so out loud in a village that has been fighting it for a decade. The vicar's wife knows who was driving the car in 2009 and has never said, and it costs her that the person who was driving comes to church every Sunday and says good morning. The girl on the post office counter sees every parcel and every registered letter in the village, wants out, and can only see one way out, which is somebody buying her mother's house.

The thing already going wrong is not the body. On your second evening you said something entirely harmless in a pub, and by Friday it has been repeated four times, the figure in it has doubled, and it is now attached to a different person. That person is the one who has to decide whether to talk to the police.

The mechanic: gossip distortion. Here the rumour system is not atmosphere, it is the plot. You do not solve the murder by finding a clue. You solve it by working out which of six versions of the same night is the firsthand one, and by reaching the person holding it before somebody who likes them less does.

The Regency of Anselm

The old king has been dead nine days. His heir is eleven. Five houses have troops within a week of the capital and every one of them has signed something with at least two of the others.

Margravine Ysoria wants the regency and can count. It costs her that the treaty which would give it to her contains a clause she agreed to in a bad week and cannot now get out of without being the person who broke it. Lord Fenn wants his daughter married into the royal house and has promised the same daughter to two families, neither of which knows about the other yet. The boy's tutor wants nothing except for the boy to survive, which makes him the only person in the palace nobody can buy, and therefore the only one everybody is watching.

The council chamber has one door and a gallery above it that three people have keys to. What has already gone wrong: the treaty was witnessed by four people and one of them is now dead, so there are three living accounts of what was actually agreed and they do not match.

The mechanic: signed terms, and couples as alliances. A treaty is clauses two parties put their names to, which the world quotes back until they lapse, and which cannot be quietly reworded afterwards. Breaking a clause does not void the treaty. It is remembered, it costs you with everyone who signed, and it becomes a fact that travels. A house is a couple: one party with two halves that want different things, and a third party has to hear yes from all of them.

What these have in common

None of them needed a feature written for it. Every one is people with days, rooms with doors, things that are unfinished, and a record of who knows what, which turns out to be a reasonable description of most stories worth playing.

If you want to build one, start at making your own world.
