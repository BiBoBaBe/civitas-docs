None of these worlds is built. Each is a sketch that names which of the engine's primitives would carry it, to make the point that the machinery has no opinion about genre.

Trapped in the game

Four thousand people logged into a game and the log-out button is gone. Nine weeks in, it has settled into a town with an economy, rents, debts and a guild that has a partial map of the boss room and has not shared it.

Vetrograd, 1961

A city cut in half by a wall. You are a translator at a trade delegation, which means sitting in rooms with people who are being watched and remembering which of them knows what. A courier who was due did not arrive, and three people have told you three versions of why.

The primitive: doors, earshot and volume. A conversation at a table is between two people; the rest of the room gets a roll, and what they can win is that something was said, never the words. A shut door hides what happened and cannot hide that it shut.

Dunmerrow

Eleven hundred people, one high street, three pubs, and a body in the reservoir. You have come back after nineteen years. On your second evening you said something harmless in a pub, and by Friday it has been repeated four times, the figure in it has doubled, and it is attached to a different person.

The primitive: rumour distortion. You do not solve the murder by finding a clue. You solve it by working out which of six versions of the same night is the firsthand one, and reaching the person holding it before somebody who likes them less does.

The Regency of Anselm

The old king is nine days dead and his heir is eleven. Five houses have troops within a week of the capital and every one has signed something with at least two of the others. The treaty was witnessed by four people, one of whom is now dead, so there are three living accounts of what was agreed and they do not match.

The primitives: signed terms, and couples as alliances. A treaty is clauses two parties put their names to, which cannot be quietly reworded afterwards. A house is a couple: one party with two halves that want different things, and a third party has to hear yes from all of them.

What they have in common

None of them needs a feature written for it. Each is people with days, rooms with doors, things that are unfinished, and a record of who knows what. If you want to build one, start at Writing a world card.
