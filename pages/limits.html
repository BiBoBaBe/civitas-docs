Everything on this page is true as of writing. It is here so that you read it before you find it.

Not built

  • Making a world from inside the app. You can import one, store one and pick one. Writing a new one is a file and a command.
  • Fuller world cards. A card covers places, people, debts and who has misheard what. A scripted world can also declare secrets with trust gates, ladders, disclosure rules, ending conditions, rooms inside buildings and doors, weekday patterns, a calendar and climate, apps, couples and terms. Bringing those to the card format is the gap most worth closing.
  • Migrating a world. Re-importing a card makes a new world. The only route into a live save is the graft tool.
  • Schema versioning. There is no migration machinery for saves. The one version field in the codebase is on the card extension block. Snapshots tolerate a missing field where it has been needed once, and nothing more general exists.

Half-built or unmeasured

  • The picture gate has a hole. A second torso fused to the first gives the body counter nothing to count. It is defended by the description alone. A second person cut off at the frame edge has also passed the check in testing.
  • Group pictures are honest at four people and no better. Identity separation holds. Named garments are wrong about three times in five at three people, and the room drifts about as often.
  • Two-person pictures including the player are behind a switch that is off. Measured at two clean frames in nine on a live scene. Three positions have been measured to a standard worth shipping; everything else is drawn another way.
  • The scene reader is only as good as the model behind it. A schema stops it naming somebody who is not in the room. It does not stop it naming the wrong person who is. Give that pass a model that follows a schema, or leave it off.
  • Resource regeneration exists, is tested, and is not wired in. Coin does not regenerate anyway. If your world counts stamina, it is opt-in and currently opted out.

Things that fail quietly

  • No graphics card means no pictures, and no error. Everything else carries on.
  • The world can be a turn behind the prose. Waiting for it would cost the narrator its whole brief, so the world lands a turn late rather than never.
  • There is a two-second budget for assembling the brief. If it overruns, the turn goes out with no world information and the model improvises. A turn that silently loses its world reads as the model going vague.
  • If no model will read a scene, the prose commits and nothing else does. The reason goes to a log file.
  • Only the last few hundred replies are tracked. Editing or deleting an older one will not rewind the world. Only a handful of owed replies are caught up at once.

Where the design knows it is rough

  • One clock and no time zones. Somebody two hours away is on world time, and the narrator does the arithmetic.
  • Cross-district acquaintance is currently still hand-wired. There is no generator yet for who plausibly knows whom across a river.
  • Names must be unique across a world. The scene reader answers with names, and "the bedroom" is five places.
  • The debt invariant checks creation, not payment. It reads the graph and cannot see the coins that moved, so it proves nothing was invented but not that the right amount was paid. The rule's own comment says so.

What is deliberately not going to change

  • The bridge has no route that returns the world.
  • The narrator will never move you, spend your money, or decide what you feel or believe. Not with a better model, and not with a better prompt.
  • Feelings have five axes. More produce an argument with noise.
  • The player receives no gossip, ever. You learn things by being told, in the fiction.
