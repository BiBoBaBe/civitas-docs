The world keeps what is true and what each person believes as two different things. A fact is a record. A belief is a link from a person to that fact, carrying their version of it and how they came by it.

Facts and beliefs

A fact is something that happened or something that is the case: she owes him forty, he tipped the bartender a hundred, two people went into the back room and shut the door. Facts are written once and never edited.

A belief is what one person holds about a fact. It carries four things:

interface Belief {
  confidence: number;      // 0..1, decays per rumour hop
  distortion: number;      // 0..1, how mangled this person's version is
  heldValue: number | string | null;   // what they think is true, which may differ from the fact
  inConfidence?: boolean;  // they were asked to keep this to themselves
}

Two people can hold the same fact and say different things with nobody lying. The clerk who is owed forty holds it at forty. The welder heard it at a bar and holds it at two hundred. Ask them in the same room and the brief gives the narrator both numbers.

Pay the debt and the fact changes. The beliefs do not. Everybody who was not there still believes you owe it, in good faith, until the news reaches them separately. Nothing had to remember to be inconsistent, because two different things are being recorded.

Provenance: how they came to know

Every belief records how it was acquired. This is the single most important property a belief has, because it decides everything that happens to it afterwards.

type Provenance =
  | { kind: "authored" }
  | { kind: "witnessed"; event: EventId }
  | { kind: "told"; by: PersonId; at: WorldTime; early?: string }
  | { kind: "inferred"; from: FactId[] };
Authored
Written into the world by whoever built it. The clerk knows about the debt because the world said so.
Witnessed
They were in the room when it happened. Full confidence, no distortion, and it never fades.
Told
Somebody told them. The record says who and when. If it was said before trust would have allowed it, the reason is recorded too, such as "drunk".
Inferred
They worked it out from other facts they hold. A person who has seen where somebody lives knows something about their money without anybody having said a word.

Firsthand knowledge never fades

A thing you saw is a thing you know for good. A thing you were told at a bar three weeks ago, by somebody who heard it from somebody, is not knowledge in the same way. If nothing happens to remind anybody, it stops being repeated and then stops being believed.

SettingDefaultMeaning
Secondhand half-life3 daysConfidence halves every three days of world time
Floor0.15Below this the belief is no longer held
Firsthand decayNoneWitnessed beliefs keep their confidence forever

This is what makes going quiet a real move. The street's sense that there is money around you fades over a fortnight. The four people who actually saw it never stop knowing.

Decay is not written back. Nothing emits an event every hour to lower a number. Current confidence is a function of the belief and the clock, so it costs nothing and cannot drift.

Who witnessed what

Witnessing is worked out from location. If you were in the same room at the minute a thing happened, you witnessed it, and a belief is created for you with witnessed provenance. Nothing stores a list of witnesses on the fact, because the list is answerable from the history at any time. See Places and doors for how rooms, doors and volume narrow that down.

The player is not a database entry

The world never decides what you believe. No belief is written for you by the simulation, you receive no rumours, and the narrator is refused when it tries to tell the world what you now know. The one exception is your own message: things you say and do are recorded as yours because you wrote them.

Names and introductions

What you call somebody is itself a belief. Until you have been introduced, the brief calls a person by their role: "the cook, works here". Once you know their name, it uses the name. The journal is rendered with the names you had at the time, so a recap of last week does not use a name you only learned yesterday.

Secrets that give themselves away

A world can declare disclosure rules: conditions under which somebody comes to hold a fact without being told. Standing in a particular place. Seeing a single payment over a threshold. Being in a room with a particular person. The belief created this way is inferred, not witnessed, so it can be a guess rather than a certainty: they saw that there was money, not how much.
