A plan is an arrangement two people made out loud, which the world then keeps. It is the only thing that outranks somebody's timetable, because it is the only thing they actually agreed to.

What outranks what

When the world decides where somebody goes, this is the order, top to bottom:

  1. A scene you are standing in with them. The story is always allowed to overrule the clock.
  2. A trip they are on.
  3. An arrangement they made.
  4. A task they took on.
  5. Their own timetable.

An errand ranks under a meeting because a meeting is two people's evening and an errand is one.

Making a plan

A plan comes from a tag in the narrator's reply, and it needs your own message to warrant it, because agreeing is yours to do:

<<plan person="NAME" place="PLACE" when="tomorrow 20:00" what="filming at his place" repeats="thursdays">>

The time is written the way somebody would say it: tonight 22:00, tomorrow 20:00, in two hours, thursday 21:00 or day 9 20:00. Weekdays resolve against the calendar the narrator has been reading all session. A phrase the world cannot read is refused rather than guessed at.

A one-off plan has an hour. A standing plan has a weekday pattern and no hour, because it is a rule about Thursdays and not a meeting on one. Each Thursday becomes a real meeting a day in advance, exactly once, however many times the world looks at it.

The lead is what keeps somebody across the city honest, since they are only reconsidered every few steps and would otherwise notice the hour after it had gone. Keeping an appointment is a committing choice: somebody on their way is not reweighing the bar against it every ten minutes.

Kept and broken

Everybody there is kept. Somebody there and somebody not is broken, and the person who waited thinks a little less of the one who did not come: a small amount of trust and a little warmth. It is a disappointment, not a betrayal. Nobody there at all is also broken, but nobody was left standing in the rain, so nobody feels anything about it.

The world never writes what you feel about being let down. That is yours. A plan is settled wherever you happen to be, because a plan you never heard about again is exactly the one worth settling.

Tasks and bounties

A task is the asymmetric version of a plan: one person asks and the other answers. It runs three ways.

  • Somebody sets you one. Accepting or refusing needs your own message. Refusing has a price: they like you slightly less for having been asked, and think slightly more of you for hearing the answer.
  • You set somebody one. Whether they do it is the world's call, weighed on how they feel about you, what it pays, and how hard the ask is. See Wants and situations.
  • You post a bounty with nobody's name on it. The money leaves your purse at once and sits in the task's own name. Whoever is available, can get there in time, and is most willing takes it. If nobody does, the money comes back.

At the deadline the world checks only what it can observe: were they at the named place between being asked and the hour, and is there a message with a photo attached in the log. Words do not satisfy a request for a photograph. People leave it late, about half an hour before the deadline, and a proof sent at the deadline counts.

Trips

Everything else on this page happens within walking distance. A trip is what happens when you say you are flying somewhere on Friday, or ask somebody three hours away to come and stay. It is not a new kind of object. It is a plan with a journey attached, so everything that knows about plans knows about trips. Four consequences follow.

A trip collides with your diary
Friday's date and a week away are both plans, and two plans that want you in two places is something the world can see and say before you book. It does not decide which wins.
A trip is lived
The journey is a real route over the map: forty-five minutes to the terminal, then twelve hours of aeroplane. The skip machinery lives it hour by hour and the city carries on without you.
A trip costs
Money leaves the purse at booking, priced off the journey. A cancelled trip does not refund the fare. Paying for somebody else's ticket is a transfer with their name on it.
A trip can be refused
Whether somebody will travel for you is answered in the order a person answers it: can they get there, have they already got something on, can they afford it or are you paying, do they want to. The refusal says which, in words somebody could text.

Somebody who has never met you is not getting on a plane for you, however warm the messages have been. Somebody who owns a business loses money by leaving it, and the world knows that from their job and their routine. A week does not cost seven times what a night costs, because leaving at all is most of the price.

When a visitor arrives somewhere small, their arrival is an ordinary fact that spreads like any other. Not until they actually arrive, though. Somebody still in their own flat is nobody's news.

Under the hood

A plan is a node with links to its parties. An invariant checks that no plan exists with nobody party to it, and that none is both a meeting and a rule about Thursdays. Whether a plan was kept is asked of the log after the hour, by checking where everybody actually was.
