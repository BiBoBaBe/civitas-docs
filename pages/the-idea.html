The world is a list of things that happened. Everything else is read off it.

Here is the whole brief for a small example world, exactly as it is handed to the model:

TIME: day 0, 19:00
PLACE: The Anchor, the Docks
  A low bar with long benches and a scarred counter.
YOU: Sam Vale
PRESENT:
  Anna Reed (mechanic)
    doing: keeping to the day
  the cook, works here
    doing: keeping to the day
  the broker
    doing: nothing in particular to do
  the welder
    doing: keeping to the day
    believes: Anna Reed owes the clerk 200
  the clerk
    doing: keeping to the day
    believes: Anna Reed owes the clerk 40
OPEN:
  [debt/escalating] Anna owes the clerk forty coins for the delivery job;
    he has stopped being patient. (29 days left)
  [secret/open] the welder has the wrong idea about Anna Reed, and believes
    it firmly enough to repeat it.

— 171/400 tokens (pinned floor 92)

Three things there are worth your attention. Anna is called Anna Reed and the clerk is called the clerk, because you have been introduced to one of them and not the other. The welder thinks the debt is two hundred; the clerk, who is owed it, knows it is forty. And the whole thing fits in 171 tokens, which is why it can be rebuilt from scratch every turn rather than carried forward and slowly corrupted.

Nothing is stored as a summary

Civitas keeps an append-only log. She walked in. He handed over four hundred. The door shut. She told him about her sister. The state of the world is worked out from that log every time it is asked. Nothing is a paraphrase of anything, and nothing is a note a model left for itself.

You will feel the consequence of that on your second evening. Swipe a reply away and the world rewinds with it: the fork happens at the moment before that reply was folded in, and the clock goes back too. The version you abandoned still exists on its own branch. A reroll is not an edit. It is a different Tuesday.

People, places and time are real

Everybody has a timetable, a home, a job, and things they want. They move on their own. The clock only advances because something said it should: a walk took eleven minutes, a scene took three, you asked to skip to Friday. It is never the real time of day.

Walk into a bar at seven and whoever is there is there for a reason you could trace. Her shift started at five. He is there because you owe him money and he has stopped being patient. Nobody was placed for your benefit.

The brief is a query, not a dump

The world is never sent to the model. Each turn it is queried against a token budget. Things that cannot be dropped are pinned first, so nobody standing in front of you is ever left out, and the rest competes for what is left over by how much it bears on this moment.

The same world at the same minute always produces the same brief, which is what makes it possible to test context assembly at all.

Then the prose is read back in

The model writes the scene. It also emits short tags the player never sees: she moved here, this situation escalated, he paid her forty, she told him the thing about her sister. A second pass reads the finished prose for what the narrator did not bother to tag, such as what came off, what each person is doing, and who had been drinking.

Every one of those is checked against the world before anything is written down.

The model proposes; the simulation adjudicates.

Tags that do not survive are rejected with a reason, and the engine can ask for one repair pass: keep the prose, fix only these. Nothing is half-applied. A verb that tries to spend your money, move your feet or decide what you feel is refused outright, always, because those are yours.

Why the split is that way round

Consistency over a thousand turns is bookkeeping, and a context window is not a ledger. Writing a room is imagination, and a ledger cannot do that. Each side gets the job it is good at.

When you notice that somebody's debt survived a week, that is not a model being careful. It is a number.

What this rules out

The simulation never writes prose, and it never decides what happened in a scene you were present for. It refuses things, records things, and moves people who are not you. Everything that reads like writing was written by the model.
