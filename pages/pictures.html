A picture is composed from the state of the world, not from the paragraph that was just written. The prose contributes the action. Who is present, where they are, and what they are wearing all come from the simulation.

An image generator asked to illustrate a paragraph draws whatever the paragraph mentions and invents the rest. If the paragraph does not mention the room, the room is made up. If somebody took their glasses off three beats ago and the paragraph does not say so, the glasses are back. Civitas avoids both by asking the model only the question it can answer.

Two separate questions

After a reply, one pass reads the prose for what changed in the world. A second pass, the director, decides what a picture would show: who is in frame, where the camera is, how close, the angle, one line about how the bodies are arranged, and where in the room. It is told plainly that it does not decide what anybody looks like, and anything it says about hair, skin, age, clothes or build is discarded.

The two are separate because they are different questions. Asked together, one pose came back for one person and everybody else fell out of the picture.

Who is in the frame

Whoever the scene was about. Failing that, the last person you were dealing with, but only if they are still in the room. A group picture takes everybody where you are, up to four. Four is measured rather than chosen: the canvas is divided into a vertical band per person, and below a certain width a band no longer holds a standing figure. The person the turn was about is never the one dropped. You are never in your own frame when the camera is your own eyes.

What they are wearing

Every person has a pinned look: a base that never changes, and a small wardrobe by situation such as home, work, a night out, casual, the gym. Which outfit a picture uses is decided from what they are doing and, above that, where they are standing. A gym is a gym for as long as somebody is in it, whatever the last paragraph said.

What the world watched somebody change into beats what their card would guess from the hour. What has come off is remembered, turn to turn and across the rooms of a flat, so a picture does not depend on the one turn that mentioned it. A removed item is removed twice: taken out of the description, and stated as something that must not appear, because leaving a tag out only makes it absent and the model's habits will put it back.

Keeping the same face

Every person has one neutral head-and-shoulders reference image, made once. At render time it steers the face and hair while the prompt controls the outfit, pose, room and framing. It is a head crop rather than a portrait, because the technique copies whatever is in the crop. Its influence is held in a narrow band, since turned up further it starts choosing the outfit and the room too. In a group picture each person's reference is masked to their own band.

One seed per scene

The random seed comes from who, where and which day, rather than being fresh per picture. Measured over six consecutive beats drawn twice: with free seeds the room changed wall by wall and clothes changed between frames; with one seed the room, the light and the clothes stayed put while the poses still read as six moments. Change the room, the person or the day and the picture starts afresh. A picture you ask for yourself gets a free seed.

The rule about who is in a picture

An image contains exactly the people it is meant to contain. Three layers enforce it.

  1. The description says so, and the canvas is never much taller than the subject, because a tall canvas is where a model stacks a second torso.
  2. A standing list of things that must not appear covers every phrasing that invites a bystander into frame.
  3. A detector runs after the picture is made, on its own worker, counting faces and bodies. Too many bodies is a stranger. Too few is two people merged. One face short is a body with no head.

A frame that fails is deleted and drawn again with a new seed, three times, and then it fails closed. No picture rather than a wrong picture. The check has a known hole, written on Limits: a second torso fused to the first gives the counter nothing to count.

Rooms are painted once, in advance

Every place in a world is painted offline and kept as files: an isometric cutaway and an eye-level backdrop, each at three times of day, with day and dusk relit from the night version so the geometry stays put. The isometric ones illustrate this site.

Pictures are best-effort

No graphics card means no pictures, and nothing else breaks. One picture is drawn per conversation at a time and the queue holds exactly one waiting job, because by the time the card is free the moment worth painting is the latest one. During a fast exchange most turns get no picture. Every picture drawn in play is captioned with who, where and when, and marked as drawn by the world, because it is not meant to match a character's portrait.
