This world is not built. It is a sketch, to show that the engine has no opinion about genre: the machinery that tracks a debt in a bar tracks a guild's map, a raid party's rules and a floor boss's deadline without any feature written for fantasy.

Four thousand people logged into a game and the log-out button is gone. Nine weeks in, the novelty is over and it has settled into something worse and more interesting: a town, an economy, jobs, rents, debts, people who are good at this and people who are not. Somebody runs the inn on the fourteenth floor. Somebody is buying up healing potions. A guild has a partial map of the boss room, drawn from what two dead scouts said, and has not shared it. One scout got out, and every version of what she saw that she does not correct becomes true somewhere she is not standing.

What is already going wrong

The scout said once, at the inn, that there is a spot by the arch where nothing comes at you. She told one person, who told another. By the third telling the confidence is down and the safe corner has become a safe stretch along the whole north wall. By the fourth, the wrong scout's name is on it. Eleven people now hold a version and three of them are wrong in a way that will get them killed. She holds it firsthand, which never decays, and she is the only one who could correct it, if she is in the room when it is said. That is Rumour doing what it does.

Before the gate, the guild put its rules in writing and everybody signed: nobody goes past the second pillar alone, the tank calls the retreat, loot is split before going in, and a safeword ends everything. Then somebody went past the second pillar alone and two people did not come back. The term remembers it, the other five think less of him, and the breaking becomes a fact that travels, so that by next week half the floor knows his word is soft. The agreement does not dissolve; ending it is the other five's move. That is Agreements.

What you would actually write

Floors are districts. The road to the gate is a street with minutes on it. The inn is a building with rooms and a door. The innkeeper has a timetable. Coin is a declared quantity, conserved. Lives are a declared quantity with a floor of zero and no regeneration. The guild's agreement is a term with clauses and a safeword. The map is a fact one person believes.
