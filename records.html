What gets written down

The world is an append-only list of events. Everything you can see on screen or in a brief is read off that list. Nothing is a summary, nothing is a paraphrase, and nothing is a note a model left for itself.

The tags

A reply changes the world only through tags. These are the thirty-three built in. A world can add its own; see Extending the engine.

TagWhat it records
moveSomebody goes to a place they can walk to. Never the player.
wentThe player went somewhere, because his own message said so.
didThe player did something the room will remember.
saidThe player told somebody something about himself.
sharedSomebody told the player an ordinary detail of their life.
toldSomebody said one of the things they keep, and to whom. Written by the scene reader.
confideSomebody tells the player something they had been keeping. Gated on trust.
revealSomebody learns something they could plausibly have heard. Never the player.
record_factSomething happened that can travel later.
appearanceWhat came off, went on, is showing, or was changed into.
doingWhat somebody is doing right now, and where in the place.
loosenedSomebody's guard came down, or came back up.
doorA door was shut, locked or opened.
feelOne person's feeling towards another shifts on one axis. Never the player's.
give, takeAn item changes hands. Never from the player without his word.
owe, settle, payA debt is made, forgiven, or paid in coin. Never the player's money.
open_thread, advance_thread, close_threadA situation starts, moves along, or ends, well or badly.
plan, trip, taskAn arrangement, a journey, or a task. Only from the player's own message.
term, pairAn agreement through its whole life, and a couple's standing with each other.
textA message actually sent, word for word.
watch, recogniseThe player was in somebody's audience, or worked out who a handle is.
advance_timeTime passed inside the reply.
retract, merge_threadRepairs only: undo a telling that never happened, or fold a duplicate situation.

What the narrator may never do

The narrator has no authority over the player. It may not move you, spend your money, undress you, decide what you feel, decide what you believe, take something off you, or claim you said or agreed to something. Each is refused unless your own message warrants it. The refusals are written as notes to a writer:

the narrator cannot move the player — let the player move himself
the narrator cannot spend the player's money — let him pay for himself
the narrator cannot decide what the player feels
the narrator cannot decide what the player believes — tell him in the prose and let him decide
only the player's own message can make a plan with him

The kinds of record

Deeds
Something you did that the room will remember. You know what you did. What is recorded is who else now does.
What you said about yourself
Filed separately from a deed, because it is hearsay about you rather than something witnessed. It carries a volume, and by default it goes to the person in front of you.
What people told you
Ordinary details, and separately, things they had been keeping. Somebody telling you a thing is their act, gated on their trust in you.
Plans and terms
Arrangements and agreements, which the world then keeps.
Texts
Messages sent, word for word. A text is witnessed by the two ends and by nobody in the room. Reading one in company is something people see you do, so the room gets the moment and never the words.

Who reaches for their phone

Unprompted texts show the general split. The world decides that somebody is thinking about you and why. The narrator writes the words. A model asked "does anybody text him?" says yes every turn, and one never told that somebody is thinking about him never has her text at all.

Only people who have your number, are not in the room, are awake at a civil hour, and have a reason: something from their day, warmth after a day apart, desire late at night. Each person at most once in twenty hours, and everybody together at most once every ninety minutes. A text nobody gets round to writing within six hours is never sent, because a checking-in message from six hours ago is not sent at three in the morning.

The journal

What happened so far is a query over the log, not a summary written by a model. Every event carries a significance from 0 to 1. Background noise is dropped, the most significant dozen are kept, and they are put back into chronological order.

It is filtered to what you witnessed and rendered with the names you had at the time. It is recomputed from the log each time, so it cannot drift, and it costs nothing.

Repairing something recorded wrongly

Scene reading is not perfect. A throwaway line gets read as a confession. A situation gets opened twice. The answer is never to edit the history. It is a small set of repair tags, adjudicated like anything else, so a bad repair is refused too. A telling can be taken back only if the person actually holds that belief and came by it by being told. A duplicate situation can be folded into the one that stands.

A correction is an event, not an edit, so the log stays honest about having been corrected. Undo proper is different: deleting a reply rewinds the world to before it and forks. See How a turn works.

Under the hood

Every event carries who could perceive it and a significance. Both are set by the verb that produced it. Witness lists seed beliefs; significance feeds the journal and the brief's ranking. The event alphabet itself is five primitives, listed on The engine.
