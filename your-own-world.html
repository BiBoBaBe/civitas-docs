Writing a world card

A world card is a JSON file that describes a world: places, people, and a few things that are unfinished. It needs no code, and it can be traded like a character card. The importer derives everything routine and shows its work.

A complete card

This is one of the two cards that ship with the engine, unabridged. Seven people, seven places, one debt, and one person who has heard the wrong number.

{
  "name": "End of the Line",
  "description": "a rain-soaked bus depot at the end of the line, run by people who have all worked there too long",
  "opensAt": "19:00",
  "opening": "Rain comes off the depot roof in a sheet and the last bus in is still ticking as it cools. The canteen smells of tea and wet coats. Somebody has left a mug on the counter with a note under it, and the cook is pretending she has not seen it.",
  "places": {
    "The Canteen":  { "public": true, "description": "steamy staff canteen smelling of tea, wet coats and yesterday's stew", "open": "05:00-23:00" },
    "the garage":   { "description": "oil-stained pit where the last buses come to leak and die" },
    "Dispatch":     { "description": "glass box of radios, rota boards and cold coffee" },
    "the platforms": { "public": true, "description": "rain-lashed concrete islands where the timetable ends" },
    "Night Office": { "description": "windowless cubby with a camp bed and a kettle that never boils" },
    "the terrace":  { "description": "leaking row of company houses behind the depot fence" },
    "Depot Yard": {
      "public": true,
      "connects": ["The Canteen", "the garage", "Dispatch", "the platforms", "Night Office", "the terrace"],
      "minutes": 4
    }
  },
  "gathersAt": "The Canteen",
  "people": {
    "Maeve Quinn":   { "role": "dispatcher",   "lives": "the terrace", "works": "Dispatch",      "knows": { "Declan Rowe": 0.8, "Bridie Nolan": 0.7 } },
    "Declan Rowe":   { "role": "mechanic",     "lives": "the terrace", "works": "the garage",    "knows": { "Maeve Quinn": 0.8, "Padraig Flynn": 0.7 } },
    "Bridie Nolan":  { "role": "canteen cook", "lives": "the terrace", "works": "The Canteen",   "knows": { "Maeve Quinn": 0.7, "Siobhan Daly": 0.5 } },
    "Eamon Lynch":   { "role": "driver",       "lives": "the terrace", "works": "the platforms", "knows": { "Declan Rowe": 0.5, "Niall Keane": 0.4 } },
    "Niall Keane":   { "role": "night clerk",  "lives": "Night Office", "works": "Dispatch",     "knows": { "Maeve Quinn": 0.6 } },
    "Siobhan Daly":  { "role": "ticket clerk", "lives": "the terrace", "works": "the platforms", "knows": { "Bridie Nolan": 0.5 } },
    "Padraig Flynn": { "role": "cleaner",      "lives": "the terrace", "works": "the garage",    "knows": { "Declan Rowe": 0.7 } }
  },
  "situations": [
    {
      "kind": "debt",
      "between": ["Declan Rowe", "Bridie Nolan"],
      "amount": 85,
      "summary": "Declan still owes Bridie for the parts he charged to her card last winter",
      "stakes": 0.65,
      "deadline": "18 days",
      "misheard": { "Maeve Quinn": 250 }
    }
  ],
  "player": { "name": "the relief driver", "at": "The Canteen" }
}

Load it with:

bun run world content/depot.world.json

The fields

The world

FieldMeaning
nameRequired.
openingThe first thing the player reads. Without one, the first turn is spent working out where you are.
opensAtThe clock the world opens at, "HH:MM". Defaults to 19:00.
gathersAtWhere people spend an evening. Worked out from the public rooms when omitted.
playerYour own person: a name, and where you start.
resources, factions, itemsWhat the world counts, bodies people belong to, and objects that can change hands.

A place

FieldMeaning
publicAnybody may walk in. A private room needs a reason to be there.
openOpening hours, "16:00-02:00". Absent means always open.
connectsPlaces this one leads to. Two-way, so write a street once.
minutesWalking time to anything it connects to. Defaults to 5.
capacityHow many people fit.

A person

FieldMeaning
lives, worksWhere they sleep and where they spend a working day. Together these derive an ordinary day.
dayAn explicit timetable, for somebody whose life is not shaped like a job.
knowsWho they know and how well, 0 to 1.
feelsHow they already feel about somebody, on any of the five axes, -1 to 1. Independent of how well they know them.
wantsWhat they are after and how badly, named by person, place, item or faction.
holdsWhat they are carrying, by resource name.
memberOfBodies they belong to, and their rank.

A situation

FieldMeaning
kindOne of the seven built in: debt, promise, grudge, attraction, contract, secret, rivalry.
betweenTwo people, the first of whom is the one under the obligation.
stakes, deadline, amountHow much it matters, how long until it comes to a head, and the figure for a debt.
misheardPeople who have heard a different number to the true one. One line gives a named person a distorted belief that would otherwise take several records assembled by hand.

What the importer derives

Leave out somebody's day and the importer works one out: asleep from 23:00 to 07:00, at work from 07:30 to 18:00, an evening from 18:30 at the gathering place. It then prints every single thing it worked out for you. A schedule you did not write is a convenience right up until somebody is in the wrong place and you cannot tell whether that was you or the tool.

It refuses rather than half-loading

A card is checked before anything is built, and you get every problem at once. If anything is fatal, it will not load. A card that half-loads is worse than one that refuses: a person whose work names a room that does not exist would simply never go to work, and you would spend an evening wondering why.

Two problems are advice rather than refusal: somebody with no home, no work and no day will never go anywhere, and a world where nowhere is public gives nobody anywhere to spend an evening.

The smallest world that works

A name. At least one place, public and connected to the others. Everybody needs somewhere they live, somewhere they work, or a day. And a player, if you want one. Six to ten people, four to eight places, and one or two unfinished situations is a good first world.

A card a model drafts

You can describe a world in a sentence and have a card generated:

bun run imagine "a rain-soaked bus depot at the end of the line"

The result is checked rather than trusted, exactly like a narrator's tags. A card naming a room that does not exist is handed back with that sentence and the model tries again, three times, then gives up and prints what is still wrong. One call, about a penny. Treat it as a first draft that is guaranteed to start, not as an author. It gives you a roster and a map; it does not give you the people.

What a card cannot say yet

A card world is a street with people and debts on it. Much of what a scripted world can declare is not yet expressible in a card: no secrets with trust gates, no ladders of wants, no disclosure rules, no ending conditions for situations, no rooms inside buildings and therefore no doors, no weekday patterns, no calendar, climate or voice, no apps, couples or terms. Anyone whose Tuesday differs from their Sunday currently needs the code route, on Extending the engine. The gap is being closed deliberately rather than pretended away.

Re-importing a card makes a new world; it does not migrate an old one. Once a card has run, it has no further say.

Cards inside pictures

A world card can be carried inside a PNG, the way character cards are, in a text chunk under the keyword civitas. The engine reads either form.
