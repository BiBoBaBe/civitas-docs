Honest limits

Everything on this page is true as of writing, and I would rather you heard it here than found it on a Tuesday.

Not built

You cannot make a world from inside the app. You can import one, store one and pick one. Writing a new one is a file and a command. See how a world gets built for the full list of which steps are buttons and which are not.

The world card format cannot express most of what Tremont is. No secrets with trust thresholds, no ladders of wants, no disclosure rules, no conditions for what would count as a situation being over, no rooms inside buildings and therefore no doors, no weekday patterns in a schedule, no calendar or climate, no apps, couples or signed terms. This is the gap I most want to close.

Re-importing a card makes a new world, it does not migrate an old one. A card is a starting state and not a second source of truth. Once it has run, the card has no further say, and the only route into a live save is the graft tool.

Half-built or unmeasured

The picture gate has a hole, and it is written down rather than papered over. A second torso fused to the first gives the body counter nothing to count. It is defended by the description alone. A second person cut off at the frame edge has also passed the check in testing.

Group pictures are honest at four people and no better. Identity separation holds. Named garments are wrong about three times in five at three people, which is the most common fault, and the room drifts about as often. At best a group frame reads as a group portrait rather than an establishing shot.

Two-person pictures including the player are behind a switch that is off. Measured at two clean frames in nine on a live scene. Only three positions have been measured to a standard worth shipping; everything else is drawn another way, which is a worse picture of the moment and a correct picture of the people.

The scene reader is only as good as the model behind it. A schema stops a model naming somebody who is not in the room. It does not stop it naming the wrong person who is. The list made the answer possible, not true. Give that pass a model that follows a schema, or leave it off.

Resource regeneration exists, is tested, and is not wired in. It is a seam rather than half a feature. Coin does not regenerate anyway, but if your world counts stamina, note that it is opt-in and currently opted out.

Things that fail quietly

These are the ones worth knowing about, because nothing tells you at the time.

No graphics card means no pictures, and no error. Everything else carries on.

The world can be a turn behind the prose. Waiting for it would cost the narrator its whole brief, so the world lands a turn late rather than never.

There is a two-second budget for assembling the brief. If it overruns, the turn goes out with no world information at all and the model improvises. A turn that silently loses its world reads as the model going vague.

If no model will read a scene, the prose commits and nothing else does. The reason goes to a log file. This happened for two consecutive replies in a live session before the fallback existed, and both turns were lost.

Only the last few hundred replies are tracked. Editing or deleting one older than that will not rewind the world. Only a handful of owed replies are caught up at once; a longer gap loses the oldest.

Places the design knows it is rough

Some of a world's interlocking lives in comments rather than in the graph. When the content says a gym owes its receptionist wages, that may be reasoning written for whoever reads the file, not an obligation the simulation can act on. The condition for a situation being over is the only thing the engine checks. The routes to it are authored guidance for the narrator.

There is one clock and no time zones. Palma time is world time. The narrator is left to do the arithmetic.

Cross-district acquaintance is hand-wired. There is no generator for who plausibly knows whom across a river. Every one of those ties was written by hand with a line of justification next to it.

Off-screen resolution is opt-in, and Tremont does not use it. Nothing resolves behind your back in the example world.

Names must be unique across the world. A reader answers with names, and "the bedroom" is five places.

What is deliberately not going to change

Some things read like limitations and are decisions.

The bridge between the chat app and the simulation has no route that returns the world. A bridge that can reach into the graph is a bridge that eventually will, and then two processes own the truth instead of one.

The narrator will never move you, spend your money, or decide what you feel or believe. Not with a better model, and not with a better prompt.

Drives are capped at six. Forty axes produce an argument with noise.

And the player receives no gossip at all, ever. You learn things by being told, in the fiction, like a person.
