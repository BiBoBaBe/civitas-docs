Places and doors

A place is a room, a flat, a building, a street or a district, and places nest. The room is the unit of sight. Who witnessed a thing is worked out from who was standing in the same room when it happened, and it is never stored.

The shape of a place

Every place has a parent. A room is inside a flat, the flat inside a building, the building on a street, the street in a district. Streets connect to each other with a number of minutes on the connection, and getting somewhere is the fastest route by time.

A place can have opening hours and a capacity. A shut place refuses you as a destination and as a shortcut through it. Closing time only stops people coming in. Nobody is thrown out at closing, because otherwise a bar is a cell.

While somebody is walking they are nowhere. Their old location ended when they left and the new one starts when they arrive. "On the road, arriving at 18:20" is a real answer, and somebody in transit sees nothing.

Who can see

You witnessed something if you were in the same room at the minute it happened. That is the whole rule. It is a question asked of the history each time rather than a flag written down, so it can never fall out of step with the history.

Who can only hear

Being in the same building but a different room is its own state: within earshot, out of sight. The brief lists those people in a separate block with the rule spelled out. They hear a raised voice and you hear them, but nobody sees through a door.

What you learn about them depends on the door. Open, and you know which room they are in and what they are doing. Shut or locked, and you know only that somebody is in there. A door works both ways.

Whether a building is one earshot or several has to be declared, because nothing about the map can work it out. A flat is its own premises: what happens in 4A has nothing to do with 1A. A gym with four rooms is one premises, and somebody on the floor knows who went into the studio even with the door shut.

A door is open unless the prose shut it, and it is open again the moment the room is empty. Locking a door is the only thing that stops a timetable walking somebody in on a scene.

Who merely notices

When two people go into a room together and shut the door, everybody left outside learns exactly one thing: who went in, where, and that the door shut. What happened inside is not theirs. That two people wanted a shut door is, and they are free to repeat it. It is a bigger deal in a public building than in somebody's own flat.

Volume: in the same room and still not heard

Standing in a room is not the same as hearing what was said in it. Anything said out loud has an audience, and the default audience is the person being spoken to.

There are two settings and no third. A thing is said to one person, or it is said to the room. Said to the room, everybody present holds it. Said to one person, only that person holds it, plus anybody the prose shows catching the words.

Everybody else in the room gets a roll. What they can win is not a fuzzy version of what was said. It is a different, true statement: she told him something she does not tell people. It carries no subject, so nothing leaks through it. The words leak only when the prose says somebody heard them.

Anything said to one person is said in confidence by default, which switches on the machinery that decides whether the listener passes it on. That is Rumour.

Arriving somewhere

When your message says where you are going, the narrator is told about the destination before you arrive: how far it is, whether it will be open, who will be there by their own timetable, and who will be audible in the next room. Without that, a narrator invents the staff, and an invented receptionist is somebody the world has never heard of.

Name a place that does not exist and the world says so. Name two that match and it refuses to pick, and asks the scene to say which.

Places you ride inside

A place can be marked as a vehicle. That marker is the whole of the feature: the vehicle is an ordinary room with its own door, and everything above applies to it unchanged. While you are in it, your location is the vehicle. What is said inside is heard by the people inside and by nobody on the pavement.

A vehicle can name a driver. That person is present, hears everything said in the back, and knows it afterwards like anything else they know. The same field gives you a carriage with a coachman or a boat with a boatman. A vehicle with no driver still works as a place to sit in and talk; the engine simply refuses to travel in it.

A place can also be marked conspicuous, from 0 to 1. This is separate from whether it gets you anywhere. It is how much it draws eyes, and it is what lets the world tell a loud car from a quiet one, or a signet ring from a plain one. If somebody has more than one, the one they take is a decision the world can see.

Under the hood

There is no "witnessed" link in the data model. An early draft had one, and nothing ever created it, because who saw a thing is answerable from co-location at the moment it happened. A stored copy of that query would be a second source of truth kept in step by hand. Firsthand knowledge is instead marked on the belief itself, as its provenance. See What people know.
