People and feelings

A person is a timetable, a set of feelings towards each person they know, a list of things they keep to themselves, and a few wants. All of it is data the world holds, none of it is prose, and most of it can only change at a rate the world sets.

Their day

Everybody has a schedule: where they are meant to be, between which hours, on which days, and what they are doing there. This is what a schedule looks like in a world written in code, for somebody who works nights:

schedule: [
  scheduleEntry(THE_GATE, hm(22), hm(6),  { activity: "on the night gate" }),
  scheduleEntry(HOME,     hm(6),  hm(14), { activity: "asleep after a night shift" }),
  scheduleEntry(ANCHOR,   hm(19), hm(22), { activity: "a drink before the shift" }),
],

An entry that crosses midnight belongs to the day it started on, so nobody teleports home at twelve. The activity is a few words the narrator sees; without one, the brief says only that somebody is "keeping to the day", which is true of everybody and describes nobody.

In a world card you can skip the schedule entirely. Say where somebody lives and where they work, and the importer derives an ordinary day: asleep from 23:00, at work from 07:30, an evening at the nearest public room. It prints everything it derived, so a person in the wrong place is never a mystery.

The schedule is the floor, not the law. It is what somebody does when nothing more interesting is pulling at them. What pulls is on Wants and situations.

Feelings, on five axes

How one person feels about another is five numbers, each from -1 to 1:

interface FeelAxes {
  warmth: number;
  respect: number;
  trust: number;
  desire: number;
  fear: number;
}

Five rather than one so that "hates him but respects him" is something the world can hold. Each feeling points one way. How she feels about him and how he feels about her are separate records, and nothing keeps them in step.

Feelings do not fade on their own. What they have instead is a ceiling on how fast they can rise, per day of world time:

AxisMaximum rise per day
Trust0.15
Warmth0.20
Desire0.15
Respect0.25
FearNo ceiling

Nothing limits how fast a feeling falls. The asymmetry is deliberate. A narrator that enjoys a scene adds a little warmth every reply, and twelve replies is one conversation. Without a ceiling somebody you met at eight would be devoted by closing time. A slow burn is a rate, so the rate belongs to the world, not the model. Fear is left uncapped because a held-back fright is not a fright.

These are engine constants, in the pacing module, and a world does not change them.

What they keep

A secret is a fact somebody keeps about themselves, with a number attached: how much they have to trust you before they will say it. The gate is their trust in you, and trust rises at most 0.15 a day, so a secret pitched at 0.45 is three evenings of work at minimum. There is no phrasing that opens it early.

Secrets ride on the character card, in a block ordinary apps ignore:

"extensions": {
  "civitas": {
    "version": 1,
    "pronouns": "she/her",
    "secrets": [
      { "id": "left-the-city", "text": "why she really left", "trust": 0.45 }
    ]
  }
}

A confidence is told once, and slowly: one a day per person, face to face. When the narrator tries to have somebody hand over something unearned, the tag is refused and the note reads like advice to a writer:

they do not trust him enough to tell him that yet (0.12 of 0.45).
let them keep it for now; it comes out when something between them has earned it

A list given up one item at a time

A secret can also be one item of an ordered list, a progression. Each item asks slightly more trust than the last, and the world releases them in order, one at a time, refusing to skip ahead. It keeps count so that a model does not have to over a thousand turns.

"progression": {
  "name": "the folder",
  "trust": 0.3,
  "step": 0.03,
  "items": [
    { "title": "one", "text": "..." },
    { "title": "two", "text": "..." },
    { "title": "twenty", "text": "", "unwritten": true }
  ]
}

At the defaults the twentieth item needs trust of 0.87, which is a lot of evenings. An item marked unwritten has a gate and no words: the words get written in play when the gate opens, so the end of a long list is not something an author had to decide in advance.

The shape is general. A witness giving up what she saw one piece at a time, a defector with a sequence of names, an apprentice being taught a craft in order: each is an ordered set with a gate per item.

When somebody's guard is down

Somebody two drinks into an evening says things they would not say sober, and a world that refuses them the words is lying about the scene. So the world tracks whether a person's guard is down, on a short clock, and lowers the trust gate by a small capped amount.

StateLastsEffect
TipsyAbout 2 hoursLowers the gate a little
DrunkAbout 4 hoursLowers it more
HighBetween the twoBetween the two
Opened up by the momentBrieflyA small non-chemical version
Everything stackedCapped at 0.3 in total

Drunk is not a skeleton key. It is the next item or two coming within reach. The narrator is shown what sits just past the lowered gate, marked as something the person cannot quite say, so a pushed confession has something true to come out as. When something does come out early, the world records that it did and why. The belief carries the reason, such as "drunk" or "he asked her to close her eyes", which is what makes the morning after possible.
