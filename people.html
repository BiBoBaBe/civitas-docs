People

A person is a day, a set of wants, five feelings about everybody they know, and a list of things they have not said yet.

Start with the smallest worked example in the engine. A creditor is owed forty coins. His timetable says he should be at the gate until six. At about eight he walks into the bar where the woman who owes him is drinking, and sits down.

Nothing scripted that. His pressure to settle unfinished business rose as the deadline came closer, until it outweighed his own working day, and the bar is simply where she is. Everything on this page is in service of that sentence.

Their day

Everyone has a timetable: where they are meant to be, between which hours, on which days, and what they are doing there. Working the taps. On the building all day: repairs, tenants, the boiler. A night shift running from eleven to six belongs to the night it started on, so nobody teleports home at midnight.

The timetable is the floor, not the law. It is what somebody does when nothing more interesting is pulling at them.

What they want

Four pressures are worked out fresh from the world rather than stored: rest, money, duty and company. Each comes with the sentence that explains it, which is the point of computing them. "He wants money because forty coins are outstanding" is a reason, and a reason is what makes somebody turning up feel caused rather than rolled.

Rest only overrides a working day once exhaustion is genuinely bad. Wanting a thing is not a reason to walk out of a shift; it is a reason to drift towards it when the day is slack. Somebody halfway through doing something does not weigh their options at all, because a person who wanders off mid-task has not made a decision, they have been walked away by a scheduler that did not know any better.

Above that sits a ladder. When somebody gets the thing they wanted, the next thing becomes live, and it is usually the thing the first one was in the way of.

Jade's studio

Jade shoots in the corner of her bedroom with the bed pushed out of frame. Solve that, by any route at all, and what she wants next is an audience, because a room was only ever the easy half of the problem. Two rungs later she wants to not need you, which is the honest end of being helped by somebody with money.

Nothing opens until the rung below it closes, and a rung only opens on a situation that resolved. Somebody whose bar closed is not now worrying about its licence.

Feelings, on five axes

How one person feels about another is warmth, respect, trust, desire and fear, each from minus one to one. Five rather than one, so that "hates him but respects him" is something the world can hold.

Each feeling points one way. How she feels about him and how he feels about her are separate records, and nothing keeps them in step.

Feelings do not fade. What they have instead is a speed limit, and only on the way up. Towards you, per day of world time, trust may rise at most 0.15, warmth 0.2, desire 0.15, respect 0.25. Fear has no ceiling.

Nothing limits how fast a feeling falls. That asymmetry is deliberate. One betrayal undoing a fortnight is exactly right, and a narrator that enjoys a scene will add a flicker of warmth every reply. Twelve replies is one conversation, and without a ceiling she would be devoted by closing time. A slow burn is a rate, so the rate belongs to the world.

What they believe

Knowing something is not a flag. It is a link from a person to a fact, and it carries their version of it: how confident they are, how mangled it has got, and what number or name they think it was.

Two people can hold the same fact and say different things with nobody lying. The clerk who is owed forty knows it is forty. The welder heard about it at a bar and says two hundred. Ask them in the same room and you get both numbers.

How somebody came to believe a thing is recorded too, and it matters more than anything else about the belief.

A thing you saw is a thing you know for good. A thing you were told at a bar three weeks ago, by somebody who heard it from somebody, is not knowledge in the same way, and if nothing happens to remind anybody, it stops being repeated and then stops being believed.

Firsthand belief never decays. Secondhand belief halves every three days and below a floor is no longer held at all. That is what makes going quiet a real move. The street's sense that there is money around you fades. The four people who actually saw it never stop knowing.

Secrets, and what it takes to hear one

A secret is a fact somebody keeps about themselves, with a number attached: how much they have to trust you before they will say it. Some people carry an ordered list, given up one item at a time, so the world can say how far along it you are instead of asking a model to keep count over a thousand turns.

The gate is their trust in you, and trust rises at most 0.15 a day. A secret pitched at 0.45 is three evenings of work, minimum. There is no phrasing that opens it.

A confidence is told once, and slowly. One a day each, because a secret is a scene and not a paragraph. Face to face. In order. If the narrator tries to have somebody hand over something unearned, it is refused, and the refusal reads like a note to a writer:

They do not trust him enough to tell him that yet. Let them keep it for now; it comes out when something between them has earned it.

A list, given up one item at a time

A secret can stand on its own. It can also be item four of twenty.

A confidence carries three things: the trust it takes to hear it, which series it belongs to, and where it sits in that series. Set those and you have an ordered list that comes out in order, one piece at a time, with each piece asking slightly more than the last.

The Folder

One character in the example world carries twenty of them under a single series name. They are things she has written about for years and never once done. As a set they are a list of fantasies, and no more needs saying about their contents than that.

What matters is the shape. She does not hand anybody a list. She does not mention that there are twenty. Item four arrives when item four has been earned, which at the default spacing is a good deal further along than item one, and the world is the thing keeping count rather than a model being asked to remember across a thousand turns. It would not remember reliably. Nothing would.

The twentieth line is marked as unwritten. The slot exists and the gate exists; the words do not. She writes it when the moment comes.

That last detail is the one worth stealing. The world holds the slot and the gate, not the sentence, so the ending of a long list is not something an author had to decide in advance.

None of this is about wants in particular. Anything ordered and withheld has the same shape:

  • A witness who gives up what she saw one piece at a time, because each piece costs her more.
  • A defector with a sequence of names, in the order that keeps him useful longest.
  • A dying man's confession, which has a first part and a last part and they are not interchangeable.
  • An apprentice being taught a craft in the order the craft has to be learned.

In each case the engine is doing the same thing: holding an ordered set, releasing one item when its gate is met, and refusing to skip ahead.

Optional depth

You do not need any of this to play. A world runs on people, places and time. Secrets and ordered lists are what you add when you want somebody to have a reason to keep talking to you for a month.

When somebody has had a drink

Trust is slow on purpose. But somebody two drinks into an evening says things they would not say sober at a bar, and a world that refuses them the words is lying about the scene.

So the world tracks whether somebody's guard is down. Tipsy lowers the gate a little for two hours. Drunk lowers it more, for four. High sits between them. There is also a short window for the ordinary non-chemical version, just after, in the dark, because you asked. Each fades gradually. Sobering up ends all of them at once.

The numbers are small and they are capped. Everything stacked together lowers a gate by no more than 0.3. Drunk and high is not a skeleton key. It is the next item or two coming within reach.

The narrator is also shown what sits just past the lowered gate, marked as something she cannot quite say, so a pushed confession has something true to come out as instead of being invented. And when something does come out early, the world records that it did, and why. Story wins, and the world remembers that it won.
