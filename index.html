Civitas

A roleplay chat forgets. A world does not.

When you play out a long story with a language model, the model is the thing doing the remembering, and it is remembering out of a transcript. For twenty turns that works beautifully. Then the money she owed you quietly evaporates. The man who swore he would never speak to you again greets you warmly. Two people give different accounts of the same night, not because either is lying, but because nothing ever decided what happened.

Civitas moves the remembering somewhere that can do it. A small simulated city holds who is where, who knows what, who owes whom, who is asleep, and what is behind which door. Every turn it writes a short brief, about half a page, and hands that to the model, which does the one thing it is extraordinary at. It writes the room and voices the people in it. Then the prose is read back in, and whatever happened becomes part of the world.

Nothing is remembered because a model happened to remember it.

What that buys you

The difference does not show up in a feature list. It shows up in the small things you notice on a Tuesday.

A shut door

Two people go into the private studio at the back of the gym and lock it. A woman racking plates fifteen feet away does not see a thing that happens in there, and the world knows she does not. Nothing from inside reaches her. But she knows perfectly well that two people went in together and shut the door, because that part happened in front of her. What she learns afterwards is whatever one of them decides to tell her.

That is one rule about rooms, and a week of consequences comes out of it. The boiler is another.

A cold flat

The boiler at 214 Holloway is dead. The quote to replace it is more than the building earns in a season, and the landlady has not said no to a developer who wants to buy the place. It is a wet autumn. Everybody in that building is cold, every day, and it is one of the few things they all have to talk about. You can write a cheque and it is over by lunchtime, except that writing a cheque that size in front of people tells them something about you that you cannot take back.

Some of it is one line of world data and still does the work. A bartender has not cashed her last three pay cheques, because she closes the till every night and knows they would bounce. A figure grows as a story is retold: forty becomes two hundred becomes three hundred and fifty, and nobody in that chain is lying. Somebody two drinks in tells you a thing they would not have told you sober, and the world records that it came out early and why. An anonymous handle on an app shows a lime under a thumbnail, which is the same lime that was in a bowl on her table.

It does not have to be innocent, and it does not have to be porn

An engine that tracks who knows what, who owes whom, and who is standing behind which door is as useful for a conspiracy as for a romance. A scheme. A debt. A favour called in. A secret that would cost somebody their job. A rumour that reaches the wrong person a day too early. Same machinery, different nouns.

The example world is a modern city with adult material in it. The engine has no opinion about genre at all, and I have sketched out a game nobody can log out of and three more worlds to make that concrete, because I would rather somebody built the thing I have not thought of.

Civitas also supports adult play, for adults, and it is off until you turn it on. How that works, including consent as an object you can point at and a safeword that always ends it, is on its own page. Nothing on this site is explicit.
