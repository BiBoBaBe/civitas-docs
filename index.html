Civitas

Civitas is a world simulation that runs next to a roleplay chat. It keeps track of who is where, who knows what, who owes whom, and what was agreed, and it hands the language model a short brief every turn so the model writes from facts instead of from memory.

Long roleplay with a language model drifts. The model is the only thing remembering, and it is remembering out of a transcript that gets summarised, trimmed and paraphrased. Money that was owed is forgotten. Somebody who stormed out is back in the room. Two characters give two versions of the same night because nothing ever decided what happened.

Civitas takes the remembering away from the model. The world lives in a small simulation with its own clock, its own map and its own record of events. Each turn it writes a brief of about half a page and gives that to the model, which writes the scene. The model's reply is then read back into the world, checked, and recorded. Nothing is true because a model remembered it. Things are true because they are written down.

What a turn looks like

This is the brief the model receives for the smallest world that ships with the engine, a bar with eight people in it and one unpaid debt. It is the whole of what the model is told about the world on that turn.

TIME: day 0, 19:00
PLACE: The Anchor, the Docks
  A low bar with long benches and a scarred counter.
YOU: Sam Vale
PRESENT:
  Anna Reed (mechanic)
    doing: keeping to the day
  the cook, works here
    doing: keeping to the day
  the broker
    doing: nothing in particular to do
  the welder
    doing: keeping to the day
    believes: Anna Reed owes the clerk 200
  the clerk
    doing: keeping to the day
    believes: Anna Reed owes the clerk 40
OPEN:
  [debt/escalating] Anna owes the clerk forty coins for the delivery job;
    he has stopped being patient. (29 days left)
  [secret/open] the welder has the wrong idea about Anna Reed, and believes
    it firmly enough to repeat it.

— 171/400 tokens (pinned floor 92)

Three details show what the simulation is doing. Anna is named and the clerk is not, because you have been introduced to one and not the other. The welder thinks the debt is two hundred and the clerk, who is owed it, knows it is forty. And the brief is rebuilt from scratch every turn against a token budget, so it cannot slowly corrupt the way a carried-forward summary does.

How a turn works follows one reply through the whole loop.

What the simulation tracks

  • Time. A fictional clock that only moves when the story moves it. Everybody has a timetable and follows it while you are not looking. See Time and routines.
  • Places. Rooms inside buildings inside streets, with doors. Who saw a thing is worked out from who was in the room. See Places and doors.
  • People. Five separate feelings towards each person they know, things they keep to themselves, and a small set of wants. See People and feelings.
  • Knowledge. What each person believes, how they came to believe it, and how their version differs from the truth. See What people know and Rumour.
  • Commitments. Plans people made out loud, tasks they took on, and agreements they signed. See Plans, tasks and trips and Agreements and couples.

What the model does and does not do

The model writes the prose and voices the characters. That is the whole of its job. It cannot move you, spend your money, decide what you feel, or decide what you believe. When it tries, the simulation refuses the change and asks for a corrected reply. The simulation, for its part, never writes a sentence of story. It records, refuses, and moves the people who are not you.

This split is the foundation everything else is built on. Principles lists the rules that follow from it, and The engine explains how they are enforced.

How you use it

Civitas has no chat interface of its own. It runs as a small service next to Marinara, which sends each reply to it and shows a World screen beside the conversation. The screen is a window onto the simulation: you can see who is where, what people intend to do next, and how they feel, without being told anything your character could not know. See Trying it and The World screen.

The example world, Tremont, is a modern city with a cast of about forty. It is one world among the ones the engine can run, and the engine has no opinion about genre. The same machinery that tracks a debt tracks an oath, a treaty or a spell. See Tremont and Writing a world card.

Status

Civitas is a working prototype in private testing. Playing works through Marinara. Writing a new world is a file and a command rather than a screen. Limits lists what is unfinished, and every page on this site says plainly when something is not built.
