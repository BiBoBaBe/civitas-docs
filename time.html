Time and routines

The world has its own clock, counted in fictional minutes since the world began. It never reads the real time of day. It moves only when something in the story moves it.

The clock

Time advances for three reasons: a walk cost some minutes, a scene took some, or you asked to skip ahead. It never advances because time passed for you at your keyboard.

A world can name its days so that the fiction and the clock agree: it can say that the story opens on day 8 and that day 8 is a Tuesday, and every timestamp on screen and in the brief is then printed on that calendar. This matters because a narrator whose header disagrees with the world's clock learns to ignore the clock.

How a turn advances time

Three things can decide how long a turn took, in this order:

  1. Marinara states a duration. Zero is allowed, and the clock does not move.
  2. The narrator tagged that time passed, such as an hour.
  3. Neither did, and the world uses its default beat.
SettingDefaultWhere it is set
Beat per turn10 minutesEngine default; a world can override it.
Longest skip14 daysEngine constant
Skip step30 minutesEngine constant
"Morning"07:00Engine constant

Time the narrator says has passed is lived, not jumped. An hour tagged in a reply runs as an hour of world: people walk, shifts end, gossip moves. If your own message says you set off somewhere, the turn ends when you arrive, so the next scene is the room you walked into rather than a street you are halfway down.

What a tick is

A tick is one step of the simulation. Long stretches are chopped into ten-minute steps with the remainder run as one short step, so a three-minute exchange still moves the world by three minutes. In one step the world:

  • turns standing arrangements into tonight's concrete meetings;
  • lets each person decide where to be, and starts them walking;
  • tires bodies, and settles anything unwatched that was due;
  • moves rumours one hop and lets old ones fade;
  • advances the clock;
  • then asks what the hour settled: which plans were kept or broken, which agreements lapsed, which tasks came due, and which situations quietly became true.

Travel does not move the clock. A journey is a departure time and an arrival time, so one person's walk across town cannot drag the whole city forward.

Skipping ahead

You can skip by a duration, until morning, until your next arrangement, or to a phrase like friday 19:30 read against the world's own calendar. The skip runs the ordinary tick in half-hour chunks and stops early when something needs you:

  • an arrangement you are part of is about to start, at the moment you would have to leave for it rather than at the hour itself;
  • somebody walks into the room you are in;
  • somebody who is fond of you sends a text.

A phrase the world cannot read is refused rather than guessed at. The refusals are plain: "say how long, or until when", "that is not ahead of now", "more than a fortnight". A skip can also be asked where it would land without living any of it.

What you missed comes back as up to a dozen lines from your own journal, read off the log rather than summarised by a model, or honestly as "nothing that concerned him".

What gets simulated in full

Everybody keeps their timetable and their appointments every tick. What varies is how much thought the world spends on them. People near you weigh their options every step. People further away are reconsidered every third step and otherwise keep to their day.

Near is measured in the web of relationships, not the street. Being in the room with you is a hop. So is owing you money, being in a situation with you, or having an arrangement with you that still stands. A creditor plotting against you from across the city is one hop away and is simulated in full. A stallholder in the next room, whom you have never met, is three hops away and is not.

TierHops from youReconsidered
Near0 or 1Every step
Mid2Every third step
FarUnreachedKeeps to the timetable

None of this is stored. The tier is computed from the graph each tick, so it cannot fall out of step with the graph.

Weather

Weather is not stored either. It is worked out from the world's seed and the minute, so the same minute is always the same rain, a reroll cannot change it, and a thousand turns of it cost nothing. It drifts over five-hour blocks rather than turn to turn.

A world declares a climate as three numbers: a typical day temperature, a night temperature, and how wet it is.

Under the hood

Fictional time is its own branded type, so a real timestamp cannot be passed where world time is expected without a compile error. The tick is a pure function from a world to a list of events, which is what lets a test run ten thousand ticks without a database and lets the adjudicator veto before anything is written.
