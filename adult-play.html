Adult play

The same machinery as the rest of this site, pointed at intimacy instead of intrigue.

Civitas supports adult play. This page describes how, in the same register as everything else here: what the mechanics are and why they exist. There is nothing explicit on it, and there is nothing explicit anywhere on this site.

It is off until you turn it on

Adult material is opt-in, per conversation, and it lives in the narrator's style contract. It is off in the example world. Not because the world is shy, but because it should be a decision somebody made rather than a default. Adult pictures are a second, separate switch, also off, and the two are independent, so you can play an adult story with entirely safe-for-work illustrations.

Everybody in every world is an adult. That is not a setting. The image pipeline refuses anything that reads otherwise, at every layer, whoever wrote the prompt.

Consent is modelled, not assumed

The distinctive thing here is that agreement is an object rather than a tone.

Terms are clauses two people negotiate and sign, which the world then holds them to. They are frozen once in force: the engine refuses to let a narrator quietly reword what somebody put their name to, and offers the alternative instead, which is to end it and draw up new terms. A safeword ends everything the instant it is spoken, costs the person who uses it nothing, and is never a breach. The narrator is handed the word in full every single time, because a narrator that has not been told the word cannot honour it. Underneath the safeword, either party can end any agreement at any moment, for any reason or none, at no cost.

Breaking a clause is the interesting case. The agreement remembers it. The other party thinks meaningfully less of you, more than they would for standing you up. And a fact is written that people can come to know: not the clause, which may be nobody's business, but the breaking of it, which is a story about your word. What does not happen is the agreement ending, because ending it is theirs to do.

The full mechanics are on Terms and couples, including couples, who are one party with two halves that want different things, and the rule that an invitation involving a third person is three separate yeses where any one no ends it. Your own yes is never something a narrator can write for you.

Desire is earned, and it is slow

Feelings towards you rise at a capped rate, a fraction of the scale per day of world time, on each axis independently. A narrator that enjoys a scene will add warmth every reply, and twelve replies is one conversation, so without a ceiling somebody you met at eight would be devoted by closing time.

Nothing caps how fast a feeling falls.

Things somebody keeps are gated on their trust in you, which means a scene that matters has to be built rather than typed. One confidence a day, each, face to face, in order. If a narrator tries to hand over something unearned it is refused, with a note rather than an error: let them keep it for now, it comes out when something between them has earned it.

Drink, and the morning after

Trust is slow on purpose. But somebody two drinks into an evening says things they would not say sober, and a world that refuses them the words is lying about the scene.

So the world tracks whether somebody's guard is down, on a short clock. A couple of hours for tipsy, longer for drunk, and a brief window for the ordinary non-chemical version. It lowers what they need before they will tell you something, by a small amount, and everything stacked together is capped well short of opening everything. Drunk is not a skeleton key. It is the next thing or two coming within reach.

Two details make it a mechanic rather than a cheat. The narrator is shown what sits just past the lowered gate, marked as something they cannot quite say, so a pushed confession has something true to come out as instead of being invented. And when something comes out early, the world records that it did and why, which is what makes the morning after possible at all.

What is off, and what she changed into

The world keeps appearance as state: what has come off somebody, what is showing, and what they changed into. What came off belongs to the flat it happened in and ends when you leave. What they changed into travels with them and ends when the day turns over, because nobody wears yesterday's clothes to breakfast.

This is why the prose and the pictures agree three beats later. Before it existed, a scene could put her glasses on the table and the next picture drew her wearing them, because nothing anywhere remembered otherwise. See Pictures.

Privacy is physical

A shut door means the woman at the weights fifteen feet away does not see what happened. It does not mean she is unaware. She knows perfectly well that two people went in together and locked it, because that part happened in front of her. What she learns afterwards is whatever one of them decides to tell her, and whether that stays with her depends on how she feels about the person it is about rather than on a flag.

The same applies in an open room. Something said quietly is between two people. Everybody else gets a roll, and what they can win is that she told him something she does not tell people, which carries no subject, so nothing about her leaks through it. The words leak only when the prose says somebody heard them. That rule exists because of a real evening in which somebody's private arrangement, told leaning in across a gym, was handed wholesale to two bystanders.

Anonymity

An app handle is not a second character. It is the same person the world already has, with all her feelings, her shifts and her side of every rumour attached to her, because the alternative is two sets of books that drift the first time something is written against the wrong one.

Two facts are tracked separately, because they are two facts: whether you know who she is, and whether she knows who you are. The second is often true first. She saw you at the bar; you never saw her.

Working out who somebody is takes a tell, written down at the same moment the handle was so it can be planted long before it is paid off, and it takes your own message, because recognising somebody is a thing you do rather than a thing done to you. A wrong guess costs nothing and leaves no trace.

A reveal does not end there. It creates something you now both hold in confidence, which makes it an ordinary secret, so it can get out by the ordinary rules to the ordinary people.

Pictures fail closed

Adult stills are their own switch. With it off, only outer layers can come off and only a short list can be shown.

With it on, the rule is unchanged. An image contains exactly the people it is meant to contain. Every frame is checked after it is drawn, on a separate worker, counting faces, bodies and poses. Too many bodies is a stranger in the picture. One face short is a body with no head on it. A frame that fails is deleted and drawn again, three times, and then it fails closed. No picture rather than a wrong picture. A known gap in that check is written up on the limits page rather than papered over.

Where a two-person arrangement has not been measured to a standard worth shipping, the system draws something else that is correct, or nothing at all, and leaves the previous picture on screen. That is a worse picture of the moment and a correct picture of the people.

Why this belongs on the same site as the rest

Nothing on this page is an adult feature with adult machinery behind it. Terms are the treaty system. The trust gate is the secret system. Appearance state is the same state that keeps somebody in their work clothes behind a bar. The door is the door.

Which is the argument the whole site is making from the other direction. An engine that knows who was in the room, what they were told, and what they agreed to is useful for a conspiracy, a murder in a village, a succession crisis, and for this. It is one engine.
