The Marinara bridge

Civitas has no chat interface of its own. It runs as a small HTTP service, and Marinara talks to it through a bridge extension. The bridge can ask for a brief, hand back a reply, and perform the player's own actions. It cannot fetch the world.

The shape of it

Two processes. Marinara owns the conversation, the model connection and the screen. Civitas owns the world. Each turn, the bridge asks Civitas for a brief, puts it into the prompt, sends the model's reply back, and shows what Civitas made of it.

Marinara                              Civitas
  |-- GET  /sessions/:id/brief ------->|   assemble the brief
  |<-- text + structure ---------------|
  |   (model writes the reply)          |
  |-- POST /sessions/:id/turn -------->|   parse, adjudicate, commit, tick
  |<-- accepted, rejected, events -----|
  |-- GET  /sessions/:id/view -------->|   everything the World screen needs
  |<-- view ---------------------------|

Every route except the health check requires a service key. There is no unauthenticated mode.

What the bridge can ask for

RouteWhat it does
POST /sessionsOpen a world, built-in or from a card, and bind character cards by name.
GET .../briefThe scene brief, rendered and structured, with an optional budget.
GET .../verbsThe tag table as prompt lines.
GET .../vocabularyThe names a constrained model may use, for a JSON schema.
POST .../turnA reply in; adjudication and committed events out.
POST .../rerollA swipe: fork before that reply and stand on the fork.
GET .../view, .../state, .../appsRead-only projections for the World screen and the phone.
POST .../walk, .../wait, .../skip, .../pay, .../text, .../planThe player's own actions, taken from the player's own screen.
POST .../appEverything the phone's apps do, one sub-route each.
GET .../text-brief, .../app-briefWhat a model is told when writing as somebody by text or under a handle.

The player's actions have their own routes, separate from narration, because the narrator may not take them. Walking, paying and texting from the World screen are your acts, and the screen is the warrant.

What it cannot ask for

There is no route that returns the graph. The comment at the top of the server says why:

A bridge that can reach into the world is a bridge that eventually will, and then two processes own the truth instead of one.

Everything the screen shows comes through views that are computed on the Civitas side and already filtered to what you could know. The bridge never walks the graph, and a bug in the bridge cannot corrupt the world.

Views

Two read-only projections serve the screen. The world view answers who knows what about you, how each person regards you, and which situations are open. The game view serves the phone, the apps, tasks, plans, the journal, the purse and the garage. Both are computed from the log on request, and both respect what you have and have not learned: a person you have not been introduced to appears as a role, and a secret appears as a count of locks, never as its contents.

Character cards

People in a world are voiced by ordinary character cards, matched by name. Matching ignores case and punctuation and nothing else. A card nobody answers to is reported rather than attached to whoever was closest, and a person with no card is voiced from the world's own description.

The card says who somebody is and how they talk. The world says where they are and what is happening. Where they disagree, the world wins, because the card may have been written for another story.

A card can carry an optional block under extensions.civitas. Ordinary apps ignore it. It holds pronouns, a pinned look for pictures, secrets with trust gates, and an ordered list given up one item at a time. Binding the same card twice is a no-op, which matters because a host reopening a chat will do exactly that.

Phone apps

Apps are engine-level, and a world chooses which to use. The engine ships four: a phone for texts, an anonymous meeting app, a cam platform and a tasks app. The list is explicitly not closed; a world may add others.

One rule governs all of them: events point at people, views point at handles. There is only ever one person behind a handle, with all their feelings, shifts and rumours attached. The handle is a mask on your screen. Nothing the bridge can read, including listings, threads and the feed, carries the name or identifier behind a handle, because one shared identifier would undo the anonymity in a single step.

Two facts are tracked separately: whether they know who you are, and whether you know who they are. The first is often true first. The second only ever comes from your own message, because recognising somebody is a thing you do. A wrong guess costs nothing and leaves no trace.
